The problem

You cannot be at every desk, every table, every hour.

i

The slow reply

A guest asks about availability on WhatsApp at 21:40. The answer comes the next afternoon. The guest has already booked elsewhere.

ii

The perfect welcome, the poor recovery

Check-in is warm and fast. But when the air-conditioning fails, nobody follows up. That is the moment the review is written.

iii

The night shift

Standards are excellent when the manager is on duty. At 23:00 a different hotel appears — and you only hear about it online.

The solution

A trained evaluator experiences your service as a real guest would.

An assigned evaluator follows the guest journey you choose, using criteria we agree with you in writing. Management receives a confidential report: what happened, when, and what to improve first.

  • 1Nine categories — response speed, friendliness, arrival, communication, service knowledge, cleanliness, complaint handling, check-out and consistency.
  • 2Facts, not opinions — every finding is time-stamped and described as it happened.
  • 3A plan, not just a score — five priorities with owners and deadlines.

This is a service-quality audit. It is not a building, safety or technical inspection.

What you receive

A confidential report your team can act on the same week.

Observations

  • 3 MAR · 14:06Greeted by name within 20 seconds of entering. Welcome drink offered; check-in completed in 6 minutes.
  • 3 MAR · 22:15Reported that the room air-conditioning was not cooling. Receptionist said a technician would come "soon". No time was given.
  • 3 MAR · 23:40No technician, no call back. Second request made by phone; the call was ended without a name or next step.
  • 4 MAR · 08:30Breakfast staff could not answer whether the omelette contained dairy and did not offer to check.
  • 4 MAR · 11:52Check-out was friendly and correct. The air-conditioning problem was not mentioned or recorded.

Five priorities

  1. Give every reported problem a time, a name and a follow-up call.
  2. Log all complaints in one shared list that the night manager checks.
  3. Train reception on one complaint-recovery routine.
  4. Give restaurant staff a one-page allergen sheet per dish.
  5. Ask about the stay at check-out and record open issues.

Advice for management. Your welcome is a real strength — protect it. The highest risk is what happens after a problem is reported, especially at night. Start with the complaint routine and the shared log; both cost nothing and can start this week. A re-audit after 60 to 90 days will show whether the change holds.

How it works

Five steps, agreed in writing.

Agree

We set the scope, criteria and dates with you in a written agreement.

Visit

An assigned evaluator experiences the journey discreetly, as a normal guest.

Verify

A second reviewer checks every finding before the report is final.

Debrief

You receive the confidential report and discuss it with us.

Re-audit

In the Excellence Program we measure again to show the change.
